Direction A · Chosen

The Swatch Journal

Editorial magazine typography + a hand-annotated layer, as if someone kept notes during an at-home trial.

Palette
Berry #7A0E3A, sage paper #F5F4EC, annotation red #C2381F, blush #F4A6C4, a five-tone skin strip
Type
Fraunces (display, soft italic) · Instrument Sans (UI/body) · Caveat (notes only)
Imagery
Wingit’s existing line-art women, framed in arches; SVG doodles with a shared “sketch” filter
UI
Quiet paper surfaces, pill CTAs, tester cards, ticks and circles as feedback

Why it wins: the visual language is the product mechanic (swatch, compare, circle the winner). It turns the existing illustrations into a system, feels premium without wealth-signalling, and still has room for warmth. Risk: annotation overuse, so notes are limited to one or two per section.

Direction B

Playful Beauty Culture

Loud grotesk caps, stickers, hot pink + yellow, Gen-Z social energy.

Palette
Hot pink #FF5FA2, highlighter yellow, black
Type
Heavy grotesk, all caps
Imagery
Illustrations as stickers, cut-outs, marquee bands

Strength: memorable, social-native. Limitation: reads as discount D2C, which clashes with premium curation and trusted at-home visits. It also sits close to Nykaa/Purplle sale aesthetics.

Direction C

Contemporary Premium

Dark, restrained, product-as-sculpture; Aesop-adjacent calm.

Palette
Espresso, bone, bronze
Type
Light serif, wide-tracked labels
Imagery
Moody product still lifes (would require a photoshoot)

Strength: instantly “premium”. Limitation: generic luxury, intimidating, and it discards the brand’s only owned asset (the illustrations). It also needs product photography Wingit can’t truthfully show yet, since we don’t know which brands it will carry.
